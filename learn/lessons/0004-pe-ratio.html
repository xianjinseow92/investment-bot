Lesson 4 · Valuation

The P/E ratio

P/E is the price divided by one year's profit per share: how many years of today's profit it takes to equal the price.

Payback years

Mei's piece earns $100 a year, steadily. If you pay X today, how many years of $100 until you've got X back?

You payYears to get it back
$5005
$1,00010
$2,00020
$2,50025

That number of years is the P/E. P = price ($2,000), E = profit per share per year ($100), P ÷ E = 20.

People pay a P/E of 40 when they expect the profit to grow. If $100 becomes $300, paying $4,000 pays back far faster than 40 years.

What P/E is not

Not the cause of the price. It's calculated from the price, like price per square foot for a flat. It describes the price.

Not a measure of growth. A high P/E can mean people expect growth, or that profit just fell (same price, half the profit, double the P/E), or hype. To see growth, look at profit over several years.

Profit per share, 3 yearsP/EReading
Shop A$100 → $100 → $10030Not growing; people pay a lot for it
Shop B$50 → $80 → $12015Growing; people pay less for it

No profit, no P/E, more risk

A company losing money has no profit to divide by, so P/E means nothing. Nobody owes a shareholder their money back. Cash a young company raised is being spent, not earned, so only future profit can pay owners. That profit may be years away, or never come. When such a company runs low on cash, it may sell new shares (dilution), which makes your slice smaller: the opposite of a buyback.

Check yourself

1. A share costs $1,500 and the company makes $100 profit per share a year. What's the P/E?

$1,500 ÷ $100 = 15: fifteen years of today's profit to equal the price.

2. A company's P/E is 30 and its profit went $100 → $100 → $100. What does that tell you?

P/E describes how much people are paying compared with today's profit. Here the profit isn't growing at all.

3. A young company raised lots of cash but makes no profit. Can it keep paying dividends forever?

Cash in the bank runs out. Only profit refills it, so without profit any dividend eventually stops.
