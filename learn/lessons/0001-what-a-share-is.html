Lesson 1 · Owning a company

What a share is

A share is a recorded piece of ownership of a company. The stock market is where people sell those pieces to each other.

Mei's shop

Mei's bubble tea shop makes $10,000 profit a year. She needs $100,000 to open more shops.

She can borrow it from a bank. Then she must pay it back with interest, however the shops do. Or she can cut ownership of the shop into 100 equal pieces and sell 40 of them at $2,500 each. That raises the $100,000, and she never has to pay it back.

The buyers now own 40% of the business. They own a slice of everything it has, and of every future year's profit, for as long as they keep their pieces. Each piece is a share.

Is it something physical?

No. A share is a record in a register saying who owns which piece, like the title deed of a flat. The deed is only a record, but what it gives you is real. Behind a share is the company's stuff (shops, machines, cash), things you can't touch (brand, recipes, software), and profits it hasn't made yet.

Why the stock market exists

Only that first sale sends money to Mei's shop. Two years later, if you need your money back, Mei won't buy your piece back. She has already spent the money on new shops.

The stock market solves this. It's a marketplace where you sell your piece to another person, and they pay you. The shop isn't involved. Because owners can always get out, people are willing to buy in, so companies can raise money from thousands of ordinary people.

Check yourself

1. When Mei first sells the 40 pieces, where does the $100,000 go?

The first sale is the only one where money goes to the company. That's how it pays for the new shops.

2. Two years later you sell your piece on the stock market. Who pays you?

After the first sale, shares change hands between people. The company gets nothing and pays nothing.

3. What is a share, physically?

It's a record, like a title deed. Paper certificates existed decades ago, and a loan is a different thing (a bond): it must be paid back, while a share never is.
