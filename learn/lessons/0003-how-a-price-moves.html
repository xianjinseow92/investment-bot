Lesson 3 · Prices

How a price moves

The price only changes when a trade happens. A trade happens when someone accepts the best price on the other side of the queue.

Three different numbers

NumberWhat it isWhen it changes
Profit per shareProfit ÷ number of sharesWhen the company earns more or less. No trading needed.
DividendCash actually paid per shareWhen the company decides to pay. No trading needed.
PriceThe price of the last tradeOnly when a trade happens

If the shop's profit doubles and nobody trades, the price doesn't move at all. Nothing updates it automatically.

The two queues

Want to sell (asking)Want to buy (offering)
Ali $2,600Dana $2,500
Ben $2,700Eve $2,400
Chen $2,800

The best offer ($2,500, the bid) doesn't reach the cheapest asking price ($2,600, the ask), so no trade happens. The last trade was $2,550, so that's the price your app shows.

News arrives: profit doubled

Frank wants a piece now, so he accepts Ali's $2,600. That's a trade, and the price is now $2,600. Grace buys from Ben at $2,700. Hassan buys from Chen at $2,800. Owners who read the news raise their asking prices or stop selling.

That's how a price goes up: more people want to buy than to sell, so eager buyers have to accept higher and higher asking prices. Bad news works the same way in reverse.

Where profit comes in

The price doesn't "know" about profit. The people do. They won't sell at the old price once a piece earns more. "The market" is just everyone trading right now. It's a crowd, not a mind, and crowds can be wrong for a long time. Day to day, prices are mostly people guessing about people. Over years, profits pull hardest, and takeovers stop a price drifting far below what a business earns.

An order is an instruction to buy or sell. A fill is an order that actually happened.

Check yourself

1. Using the queues above, Eve wants a piece right now. What does she pay?

To buy right now, she has to accept the cheapest asking price, Ali's $2,600.

2. The shop's profit doubles overnight, but nobody trades. The price…

The price is just the last trade. It only moves when a new trade happens.

3. After bad news, many owners want to sell right now. The next trades happen…

Eager sellers have to accept lower and lower offers in the buying queue.
