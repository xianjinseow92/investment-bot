How shares work

A share is a recorded piece of ownership: a claim on a slice of everything a company owns and earns, for as long as you hold it. Only the first sale (IPO) sends money to the company. After that, shares trade between people.

Four ways owning pays you

DividendCash per share, out of profit
BuybackCompany cancels shares → your slice gets bigger (pays you only if you sell)
TakeoverEvery share bought at the agreed price; your broker swaps it for cash
SellingTo another person, at whatever price you agree

Opposite of a buyback: dilution, where new shares are issued and your slice gets smaller.

Three numbers, never mixed up

Profit per shareChanges with the business
DividendChanges when the company decides
PriceChanges only when a trade happens

How a price moves

Bid = best offer in the buying queue. Ask = cheapest price in the selling queue. A trade happens when someone accepts the other side's best price. More eager buyers mean trades at rising asks; more eager sellers mean trades at falling bids. An order is the instruction; a fill is the trade that happened.

P/E ratio

Price ÷ profit per share = years of today's profit that equal the price. It describes the price: it doesn't cause it and doesn't measure growth. Growth shows in profit over several years. No profit means no meaningful P/E.
