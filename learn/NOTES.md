# Teaching notes

How the owner likes to learn, and what to keep in mind. Read before every lesson.

- **Go slow.** One idea per message. The first attempt crammed ten ideas into one message and caused real frustration; the owner asked explicitly to slow down.
- **Mechanisms over definitions.** The owner asks "how does this actually work?" and rejects loose wording. Example: "your piece becomes worth more" was rightly challenged with "the price only moves when a trade happens". Keep profit per share, dividend and price strictly separate.
- **Running example:** Mei's bubble tea shop, 100 pieces, $10,000 profit a year ($100 per piece). KOI is the would-be buyer. Ali, Ben, Chen (sellers) and Dana, Eve (buyers) are the queue. Keep reusing them.
- **Name the confusion first.** When the owner is stuck, restate what you think the gap is and ask if that's it, before explaining again. The owner asked for this and it worked.
- **"What's in it for me?"** is the owner's anchor question. Tie every concept to it.
- **Branching:** each answer tends to spawn several new questions. Park every new question in `QUESTIONS.md` (tagged now / later) instead of answering them all at once, and close one loop before opening the next.
- **Check questions first.** Ask the owner to answer the previous check questions from memory before new material. Recall is what makes it stick.
- Some messages are voice-typed; read them charitably and ask if a sentence is unclear.
- The owner is hard on themselves when stuck ("I'm too dumb"). Confusion has mostly come from explanations going too fast; say so plainly and keep going.
